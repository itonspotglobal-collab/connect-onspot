Redesign the Manifesto page (/manifesto) — vibe direction, not literal
spec. Trust your own design judgment on exact execution.

THE FEELING: this should read like a personal letter, not a marketing
page. Quiet confidence, not a pitch. Someone should land on this page and
feel like they're being let in on something honest, not sold something.
Think: a founder's private note that happened to become public, not a
"brand values" section.

WHAT'S CLUTTERED RIGHT NOW, FIX THIS:
- Too many things competing for attention above the fold — a nav pill,
  an eyebrow tag, the giant headline, AND a floating "WRITTEN FOR"
  module all fighting each other in the same visual space. Quiet all of
  that down to ONE loud moment (the headline) and let everything else
  recede.
- The eyebrow label currently just repeats the headline word-for-word
  ("THE ONSPOT MANIFESTO" above "The OnSpot Manifesto") — replace with
  something that doesn't restate the title, or drop it.
- The "WRITTEN FOR" sidebar element feels like a UI widget bolted onto a
  piece of writing. A manifesto doesn't need metadata chrome — if
  authorship needs to appear, it belongs as a quiet signature at the very
  END of the piece, like how someone actually signs a letter, not as a
  floating label competing with the opening.

WHAT STAYS, DO NOT TOUCH:
- The large, tight-leading, overlapping headline treatment ("The OnSpot
  Manifesto" stacked with intentional letter/line overlap) — that's the
  one deliberate, confident visual statement on the page. Keep it.

THE HANDWRITTEN TOUCH: use a refined script/handwriting webfont
SPARINGLY — a few emphasis lines within the body ("We don't accept
that.", the closing "Work Without Limits.") and the sign-off signature at
the end ("— Nur Laminero, Co-Founder & CEO"). Not the whole body text —
that would hurt readability at this length. It should feel like a
handwritten note dropped into a typeset letter, not a page written
entirely in cursive.

PACING: let this breathe. Generous whitespace between paragraphs, a
comfortable single-column reading width (not full-bleed sprawling text),
nothing decorative competing with the words themselves. The restraint IS
the design — if a screen could be mistaken for a template, that's the
signal something's still too busy.

BRAND: stay within the existing system — navy, purple, gold used
sparingly (gold has one job: the one or two moments that need emphasis,
not decoration), Bricolage Grotesque for display type, Inter for body,
the real logo component in the nav (not plain text).

Also fix: the top-left "OnSpot" text should be the actual logo component/
asset already used elsewhere in the site, not plain rendered text.
